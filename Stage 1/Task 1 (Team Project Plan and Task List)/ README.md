## B. Project Scope and Learning Goals (MJ)
1. Target Learners

   Beginners who have little to no experience in algorithms, data structures, and connectionist AI fields. The specific study guide will have step-by-step explanations from a minor scope to a larger scope, provide exercises or questions on each chapter, give the visualization of a diagram or a chart, and a tutorial on a mini coding project that implies the three main subjects. Thus, beginners can study progressively. In assumption, beginners are able to perform basic arithmetic formulas, understand mathematical relationships, and have used a computer in their life.

2. Learning Goals 

   Learners should have either knowledge and practical skills by the end of the learning session. Basic concepts such as definitions and implementations, simple programming Python, problem solving skills based on algorithms and data structure, and knowledge of AI should be mastered by learners. Technical skills are also improved after doing some basic projects on the study guide.

## Planned Learning Resources and Mini Lecture (Eva)
- Shared Markdown Content
   + Each topic will be divided into chapters, organized from the basic to advanced. As along the way learners will learn key terms and fundamental concepts before moving on to more complex ideas. This structure will help learners grasp the information presented in PPT slides and video tutorials.
   + Each chapter will include detailed explanation and fundamental concepts that are essential for further learning. Short quizzes at the end of each chapter can help learners evaluate the materials.
   + To help beginners understand difficult concepts, the learning materials will include every-life examples, step-by-step explanations, and practical coding demonstrations.
    + The mini projects will allow learners to apply their study and evaluate how they understand the concepts in the study guide. 
- Study Guide and Test Bank
  + The study guide will follow the same as shared Markdown to maintain consistency and avoid confusing learners.
  + Practice activities include multiple choice questions, Python coding exercises, and step-by-step problem solving exercises. At the end of the study guide, a mini quiz game will review the fundamental concepts covered throughout the learning materials.
  + Answers and explanations will be provided to help learners evaluate their understanding, identify mistakes, and review concepts they find difficult.
  + We have also developed a mini-quiz game with multiple stages, featuring questions and explanations to help learners review.
- PPT Slides
   + The PPT slides will include visual elements to aid beginners in understanding, including visual representations of how Algorithms, Data Structures and Connectionist AI works. Diagrams will be shown where needed to help understanding but minimized to not be overwhelming for beginners.
   + Slides will be designed according to theme and neatly, keeping it short and easy to digest instead of long and informative which can be overwhelming and demotivate new learners.
   + FAQ will be included to help in questions unanswered, and an end of chapter quiz (with answers) will be included to test the learners' knowledge.
- Video Tutorials
   + The video will only include how the mini project will play out as it is the most convenient way to understand how each concept is applied. Mini projects include Bubble Sort, BFS Maze Explorer, and Neural Network Classifier.
   + In the video, there will be demonstrations narrated by our group’s mascot. To maintain learners' engagement, the video won't last for more than 10 minutes. In that 10 minutes, the video will cover all the necessary problems addressed by each project. A detailed step-by-step programming with the input and output explained.
   + With the video, viewers can follow the programming process more easily and can always come back to the video when needed.
- Live Mini Lecture
   + The mini lecture focused on each chapter and the sub chapters. Explaining only the fundamentals and key ideas in the learning resources. More important contents will have detailed explanations, while complex ones will have a short and dense explanation to help learners.
   + During the mini lecture, questions will be posed to the audience to encourage participation and engagement. These questions will encourage learners to predict what comes next and apply their understanding of the topics.
   + By the end of the lecture, the audience should have a grasp on the key concepts covered and are able to apply their knowledge when completing the mini projects.

##Workflow, Schedule, & Coordination

1. Workflow

- Step 1: Study and investigate the content
All team members will review the relevant course materials and identify the key concepts, terminology, and examples related to Algorithms, Data Structures, and Connectionist AI. Each member will research their assigned topics using reliable textbooks, lecture notes, and academic or educational resources. Members will record useful references and clarify difficult concepts with one another or the teacher.
- Step 2: Develop the shared Markdown content
The team will create a consistent Markdown structure for the study guide, including learning objectives, concept explanations, worked examples, diagrams where appropriate, practice questions, and references. Each member will draft their assigned sections using the shared format. The team will agree on terminology, formatting, and the expected level of difficulty before combining the sections.
- Step 3: Check and consolidate the content
Members will review one another's work for technical accuracy, clarity, completeness, and suitability for beginners. Algorithm explanations and data structure operations will be checked using worked examples or pseudocode. Connectionist AI explanations will be checked for correct descriptions of neural networks, neurons, weights, activation functions, and learning processes. Any unclear or disputed points will be discussed before the content is approved.
- Step 4: Prepare the study guide and practice materials
After the Markdown content has been reviewed, the team will organize it into a coherent study guide. Each major topic will include explanations, examples, and practice questions where appropriate. A test bank will be developed to help learners check their understanding, with answers and brief explanations for self-assessment.
- Step 5: Collect feedback and revise
The team will test the study guide by asking members to use it as beginner learners and attempt the practice questions without relying on the answers. Feedback will be recorded, and corrections will be made to the shared Markdown source before updating the study guide and any related slides or video tutorials.
